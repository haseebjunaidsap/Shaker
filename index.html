Module 01 · 50 minutes

What AI really is

You do not need to know how an engine works to drive a car. You do need to know what the pedals do, how far the tank goes, and when to brake. This module is the pedals.

After this module you can
  • Explain AI, machine learning, generative AI and agents in plain words
  • Describe how a language model produces an answer, step by step
  • Use the seven terms that come up in every AI meeting
The one analogy to remember. Modern AI is a brilliant new intern. It has read millions of books, contracts and accounting standards, types at lightning speed and works 24/7. But it has never been inside your company, does not know your numbers, and will sometimes answer confidently when it should have said "I don't know". You would never let a new intern post a journal without review. Treat AI the same way.

The family tree, from broad to specific

Artificial Intelligence

Computers doing tasks that normally need human thinking

The umbrella term. It covers everything from the spam filter in Outlook to Copilot.

The word "Finance". It covers AP, tax, treasury and FP&A, which do very different work.

Machine Learning

Learning rules from examples instead of being programmed

Nobody writes "if vendor = du then GL 6120". The system studies 10,000 past invoices and works out the pattern itself.

A junior who learns GL coding by studying how the team coded last year's invoices, not by reading the manual.

Generative AI · LLM

AI that writes new text, tables, formulas or images

A Large Language Model (the brain inside Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) has read a huge share of the world's text and learned how language, and the knowledge inside it, fits together.

Your phone's autocomplete, scaled up a million times, so it can finish an entire memo instead of one word.

AI Agent

AI that takes actions toward a goal, not just answers

An agent can open files, run a reconciliation, draft and queue emails, or update a tracker, following a goal and rules you set.

An intern who tells you which invoices are overdue, versus one who also drafts the chasers, queues them for your approval and logs the replies.

How a language model produces an answer

Strip away the magic and an LLM does one thing: given some text, it predicts the most likely next word, adds it, and repeats. It does this thousands of times per answer. Try it below: click a word to add it, and watch how the probabilities change with context.

Next-word prediction · illustrative probabilities

Step 1 · Training

It reads, a lot

The model reads trillions of words of books, websites, code and papers, and adjusts billions of internal settings ("parameters") until it predicts well.

An articled clerk who read every working paper in the firm's archive for 40 years. They never memorised a file, but they developed very good instincts.

Step 2 · Fine-tuning

It learns manners

People then teach it to follow instructions, be helpful, refuse harmful requests and format answers well.

The same clerk going through the firm's induction: how we write to clients, what we never say, how we lay out a memo.

Step 3 · Your prompt

It answers your brief

Your instruction plus any files you attach become the starting text. The model predicts the reply word by word. Copilot also searches your emails and files first, so it can answer from your own data.

Handing that clerk a client file and a clear brief. The better the file and the brief, the better the work.

Module 02 · 40 minutes

Strengths, limits and the jagged frontier

AI is superhuman at some tasks and surprisingly poor at others that look similar. Researchers call this the "jagged frontier". Your job is to learn where the edge is, so you use AI hard on one side and check carefully on the other.

After this module you can
  • Predict which finance tasks AI will do well and which it will get wrong
  • Recognise the four common ways AI fails
  • Apply a simple traffic-light rule for how much to check

A GPS that is brilliant on Sheikh Zayed Road but occasionally sends you down a closed service lane in Al Quoz. You keep using GPS. You also keep your eyes on the road, especially in unfamiliar areas.

The evidence. In a Harvard Business School and BCG experiment, 758 consultants with AI finished tasks inside AI's abilities faster and to a higher quality. On a task just outside those abilities, the consultants using AI were more likely to get the answer wrong, because they trusted output that looked convincing. See the paper in the library (module 11).

Inside the frontier: use it hard, still review

  • Summarising long contracts, SOWs, audit letters and policies
  • Drafting emails, memos, variance commentary and board narrative
  • Writing and explaining Excel formulas, Power Query steps and pivot setups
  • Reading invoices, receipts and PDFs and pulling out fields
  • Translating between Arabic and English, and adjusting tone
  • Spotting odd items in a list: duplicates, outliers, round amounts, weekend postings
  • Turning messy notes or a meeting transcript into action lists

Outside the frontier: never trust without checking

  • Arithmetic done "in its head" in a chat. Let Excel do the maths; let AI write the formula.
  • Citing exact tax law articles, IFRS paragraph numbers or FTA decisions
  • Recent events or rule changes, unless it searched the web and shows sources
  • Your company's policies, unless you point it to the actual document
  • Judgement calls: provisions, revenue cut-off, impairment, going concern
  • Counting things precisely in long documents ("how many invoices are listed?")

The four ways AI fails, with finance examples

Failure 1

Confident invention

Asked for the UAE Corporate Tax article on interest limitation, it gives an article number that looks right but is wrong.

A trainee who guesses rather than admits they don't know.

Defence: ask for sources, open them, cite the original.

Failure 2

Silent arithmetic slips

It sums 40 cost centres in a chat and is off by AED 12,400.

A tired person adding on a napkin.

Defence: calculations live in Excel. Reconcile AI tables back to the GL total.

Failure 3

Missing context

It drafts a collections email that is perfect for a private client but too blunt for a government entity you have a 10-year relationship with.

A consultant from head office who has never met the client.

Defence: give context in the prompt; review tone for key accounts.

Failure 4

Eager to please

You ask "this accrual is fine, right?" and it agrees. Ask "what is wrong with this accrual?" and it finds three issues.

A junior who tells the boss what the boss wants to hear.

Defence: ask it to challenge you, not confirm you.

Traffic-light rule: how much to check

Green: skim

Internal drafts, meeting summaries, email polishing, explaining a concept to yourself.

Read once before using.

Amber: verify

Numbers in reports, contract summaries, formulas, anything going to your manager or a client.

Tick key figures to source. Test formulas on two rows.

Red: human decides

Tax positions, journals, payments, audit responses, accounting judgements.

AI may draft or research. You decide, document and sign off.

Why now: what is changing in the UAE

E-invoicing · Peppol PINT AE

PDF invoices are being replaced by structured data

Businesses with annual revenue of AED 50 million or more had to appoint an Accredited Service Provider by 30 October 2026 and must go live on 1 January 2027. Businesses below that threshold follow on 1 July 2027, and government entities on 1 October 2027. Failing to implement or appoint an ASP costs AED 5,000 per month of delay.

Moving from posting cheques to bank transfers. The invoice becomes data a machine reads instantly, which is exactly what automation needs. Clean master data now makes this painless.

Corporate tax · 9%

A new annual workpaper cycle

Returns and payment are due within nine months of the tax period end, so a December 2025 year-end was due by 30 September 2026. Records must be kept for at least seven years. Every repeatable review in that cycle is a candidate for an AI-assisted first pass.

A new statutory audit you now run in-house every year. The second year is always faster if you systemise the first.

Module 04 · 90 minutes · the most important module

Prompting masterclass

Prompting is delegation. The skills that make you good at briefing a junior (context, a clear deliverable, an example, a deadline) make you good at prompting. Most disappointing AI output comes from a one-line brief.

After this module you can
  • Write a complete brief with the six-part framework
  • Use seven techniques that professionals rely on
  • Build and reuse a personal prompt library

Ordering at a tailor in Karama. "Make me a suit" gets you a suit. "Navy, slim fit, two buttons, for a client presentation in 40-degree heat, here is a photo of one I like" gets you your suit.

The six-part brief: R-T-C-F-E-C

R · Role

Who should it act as?

"You are a senior IFRS accountant at an IT services company."

Sets vocabulary and depth, like choosing which colleague to ask.

T · Task

The exact deliverable

"Draft variance commentary for September vs budget."

One task per prompt works best.

C · Context

Everything a stranger needs

Entity, period, currency, audience, the data, what you already know.

This is where most prompts are too thin.

F · Format

Shape of the answer

"Table with 4 columns", "3 bullets, max 40 words each", "formal English".

E · Example

Show what good looks like

Paste last month's approved commentary or a model email.

The single most powerful upgrade.

C · Check

Make it flag its doubts

"List assumptions and anything you are unsure of."

Turns a confident guess into a reviewable draft.

ICAEW teaches a very similar five-part structure (Task, Persona, Context, Example, Format) in its generative AI courses for accountants. The extra "Check" step here makes the AI tell you where it might be wrong.

Weak brief

Explain the variance in IT costs.

Result: a generic paragraph about why IT costs rise. Useless.

Strong brief

You are a finance business partner at an IT services company in Dubai.
Task: draft variance commentary for Sept 2026 vs budget for the CFO.
Context: Software licences AED 412k vs budget 330k (+82k). Notes: Microsoft EA true-up 55k booked in Sept; 12 new delivery consultants onboarded for the Riyadh bank project, each needing licences.
Format: 3 bullet points, max 60 words, say whether one-off or recurring.
Check: list any assumption you made.

Result: specific, ready to edit in under a minute.

Module 06 · 45 minutes

The logic of automation

Before building anything, you need to answer two questions: what kind of automation fits this task, and is the task worth automating at all? Accountants already have the instincts for both, because they are the same questions you ask about a new control.

After this module you can
  • Tell rules-based automation, AI assistance and agents apart
  • Score any task for automation potential
  • Map a process on one page before you automate it

Three kinds of automation

1

Rules-based

Same steps, every time

Excel formulas, Power Query, Power Automate flows. The computer follows exact instructions you defined.

A vending machine, or a standing order at the bank. Perfect and tireless when the input is exactly as expected; stuck when anything differs.

Use for: combining files, scheduled reports, moving attachments, reminders.

2

AI-assisted

Handles variety, needs a human

Copilot reads messy inputs (different invoice layouts, free-text emails, contracts) and produces a draft or extraction you review.

A good barista. "Flat white, oat milk, half sweet, extra hot" is no problem, but you still taste it before serving it to the CFO.

Use for: extracting from PDFs, drafting commentary, classifying expenses.

3

Agentic

Pursues a goal across steps

An agent combines AI judgement with tools and permissions: it decides the next step, takes actions, and asks you at checkpoints.

A contractor with a job description, a system login, a spending limit and a manager to escalate to.

Use for: running a reconciliation end to end, collections follow-up, IC dispute triage.

The best solutions combine all three. In Recipe D, a rule (new email with attachment) triggers AI (read the invoice), and the result follows rules (confidence below 80% goes to you). Agents add judgement on top.

Should I automate this? Score a task

Recipe A · Copilot in Excel & Word

Recipe B · Power Query

Combine all GCC entity trial balances with one click

MediumSaves ~4 hrs / monthBuilt into Excel
BeforeOpen six files, copy-paste, look up FX rates, fix columns that moved, rebuild the pivot.
AfterDrop new files in a folder and press Refresh All. Every step replays in seconds.
Folder of TB exports→Get Data from Folder→Add entity + FX→One AED table→Refresh monthly
  1. Create a OneDrive or SharePoint folder. Save each entity's trial balance export (UAE, KSA, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Bahrain) with the same columns and the entity code in the file name.
  2. In Excel: Data → Get Data → From File → From Folder. Choose the folder, then Combine & Transform.
  3. Create a small table of month-end rates to AED and merge it on the currency column. Add a column: Amount × Rate.
  4. Stuck? Ask Copilot: "Write Power Query M code that extracts the entity code from Source.Name, which looks like TB_KSA01_2026-09.xlsx, and explain each line."
  5. Load to a sheet and build the pivot. Next month: replace the files, press Refresh All.

A recipe card in the kitchen. You write the steps once; whoever cooks next month follows the same card and gets the same dish.

Control: add a check row so the total of each entity's TB equals zero, and the consolidated TB equals the sum of the entities.

Design Agent B: "Intercompany Mismatch Agent" (with IT, Copilot Studio)

Intercompany is the classic month-end headache in a multinational IT services firm: the UAE entity books a recharge from the delivery centre at one rate on one date, and the counterpart books it differently. This agent design is your specification to take to IT. You own the process; they help build it.

GoalAll intercompany balances with group entities agreed, or explained, by WD3.
TriggerWD2 at 09:00, after both sides have posted.
Inputs & knowledgeIC balance exports from each entity, IC agreement and recharge policy, month-end FX rates.
ToolsReconciliation of two tables; Outlook drafts; a SharePoint list as the IC issue log.
What it doesMatches both sides, classifies each difference (FX, timing, missing invoice, wrong account), and drafts a query email to the counterpart's accountant.
Human checkpointsYou approve every email before it is sent. You decide every adjustment.
PermissionsRead-only access to ledger exports. No posting rights. Email only to internal group addresses.

An air traffic controller for intercompany. It sees every plane on both radars, flags the ones that don't line up, and radios the right tower, but the landing decision stays with the human in charge.

Agent guardrails, non-negotiable. Start every agent read-only. Never give an agent rights to post journals, release payments or change vendor bank details. Give every agent a named human owner, a log of what it did, and a way to switch it off. Review its outputs on a sample basis every month, exactly as you would review a junior's work.

Data traffic light: what goes where

Green · public

Published laws, FTA guides, IFRS concepts, general Excel questions.

Any tool, including free web chatbots.

Amber · internal

Trial balances, invoices, contracts, client names, budgets, emails.

Microsoft 365 Copilot only, under your company login.

Red · restricted

Salaries and personal data, bank account details, passwords, unannounced deals, anything your policy marks restricted.

Follow company policy. If unsure, don't, or mask the data first.

Documenting an automation for audit

Keep a one-page record per automation

  • Purpose and process it supports
  • Owner and backup owner
  • Trigger, inputs, outputs
  • Human review step and who performs it
  • Control totals and exception handling
  • Date built, last changed, and who approved it

Keep evidence each period

  • The run log or report (reconciliation report, invoice log)
  • Exceptions and how each was resolved
  • Reviewer sign-off with date

CCTV plus a visitor book. Nobody watches every minute, but you can always show who came in, when, and what they did.

Red lines. AI does not post journals, approve payments, change vendor bank details, file tax returns, or sign off accounts. A human with the right authority does, every time.
